# PDPA Data Subject Rights (DSR) & Breach Notification Policy

This document outlines Sopet's formal process for handling Data Subject Rights (DSR) requests and responding to personal data breaches in accordance with the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) of Thailand.

## 1. Data Subject Rights (DSR) Process

Under the PDPA, users (Data Subjects) have specific rights regarding their personal data. Sopet is committed to fulfilling these requests within **30 days** of receipt.

### Supported Rights

- **Right to Access:** Users can request a copy of their personal data.
- **Right to Rectification:** Users can request correction of inaccurate data.
- **Right to Erasure (Right to be Forgotten):** Users can request deletion of their data.
- **Right to Restriction of Processing:** Users can request a temporary halt to processing.
- **Right to Data Portability:** Users can request their data in a machine-readable format.
- **Right to Object:** Users can object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing.

### DSR Handling Workflow

1. **Receipt:** DSR requests can be submitted via the Support email (e.g., \`sopetofficial@gmail.com\`) or through the Privacy Settings in the user dashboard.
2. **Verification:** The identity of the requester must be verified (e.g., via OTP to the registered phone number or email from the registered address) before processing the request.
3. **Assessment:** Determine if the request is valid and if any exceptions apply (e.g., legal obligation to retain data for accounting purposes).
4. **Fulfillment:**
   - **Access/Portability:** Generate a JSON or CSV export of the user's data from the database.
   - **Erasure:** Perform a soft-delete or anonymization of the user record, ensuring compliance with retention policies.
   - **Rectification:** Update the relevant records in the database.
5. **Notification:** Inform the user that their request has been fulfilled or explain the reasons if the request is denied or delayed.
6. **Record Keeping:** Log the DSR request, the actions taken, and the completion date in the privacy compliance register.

## 2. Personal Data Breach Notification Process

A personal data breach is a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to, personal data.

### Breach Notification Timeline

- **Internal Reporting:** Any employee or contractor who suspects a data breach must report it immediately to the Security/Privacy team.
- **PDPC Notification:** If the breach poses a risk to the rights and freedoms of individuals, Sopet must notify the Personal Data Protection Committee (PDPC) **without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours** of becoming aware of it.
- **Data Subject Notification:** If the breach is likely to result in a **high risk** to the rights and freedoms of individuals, Sopet must notify the affected individuals without undue delay.

### Breach Handling Workflow

1. **Detection & Initial Assessment:** Following the Incident Response Runbook, determine if personal data is involved.
2. **Risk Evaluation:** Assess the severity of the breach based on:
   - Type of data involved (e.g., financial info, contact details).
   - Volume of data or number of individuals affected.
   - Potential consequences for the individuals (e.g., identity theft, financial loss).
3. **Containment:** Take immediate steps to stop the breach and secure the data.
4. **Notification Decision:**
   - **Risk exists:** Prepare PDPC notification.
   - **High risk exists:** Prepare PDPC notification AND Data Subject communication.
5. **Drafting Communications:**
   - Ensure the notification includes the nature of the breach, the name and contact details of the DPO/Security contact, likely consequences, and measures taken to address the breach.
6. **Post-Breach Review:** Conduct a post-mortem to identify root causes and implement preventive measures to avoid recurrence. Update this policy as needed.
